The ferry kiosk was closed when Farah arrived. Dawn had reached the water but was taking its time with the pavement. Jo stood beside the shutter, holding an empty travel mug.

“You’ve come equipped,” Farah said.

“I thought I’d give the coffee somewhere to go.”

Jo tilted the mug toward her. Farah inspected its emptiness with appropriate gravity.

“Very spacious.”

“Nothing but the best.”

Farah stood beside her sister. For a moment they both faced the shutter, as though excellent manners might persuade it to rise. Then Farah brought their late aunt’s letter out from beneath her hand. The envelope was still sealed.

Jo’s eyes went to it.

“You brought it.”

Farah nodded and held it out, leaving the space between them for Jo to cross.

“Not today.”

Jo kept her free hand against her elbow. Farah lowered the letter without putting it away.

“All right.”

They stood a little longer. Jo looked into her mug again.

“It hasn’t improved,” Farah said.

“I’m allowing it time.”

Farah smiled. Jo almost did, then glanced at the envelope.

“You haven’t opened it?”

“No.”

Jo studied the sealed edge. She knew the letter existed; that was still the extent of what she knew about it. Farah held it loosely by one corner, clear of the seal.

The shutter stayed down. Jo shifted the empty mug to her other hand, freeing the one nearest Farah.

“Here.”

Farah looked at the offered hand before placing the letter in it. Jo took hold; Farah let go. Neither made the exchange brisk enough to pretend it was nothing. Jo rested her thumb on the envelope’s face and left the flap alone.

Farah rubbed her now-empty fingertips together.

“Are you going to leave town?” Jo asked.

Farah looked across the water, then back at her sister.

“Not today.”

Jo gave a small nod. She lifted the mug, caught herself before taking a useless sip, and lowered it.

“Excellent breakfast,” Farah said.

“Hard to overdo it.”

Their shoulders touched as Jo shifted closer. The dawn was beginning to warm the shutter. Farah stayed beside her, and Jo held the mug in one hand and the letter, still sealed, in the other.
